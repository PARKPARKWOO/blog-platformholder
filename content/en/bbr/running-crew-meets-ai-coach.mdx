The crew schedule is fixed. Where strength work belongs between those runs is still your decision.

Start by dropping one sentence: **“Strength work prevents running injuries.”** The current evidence cannot support that promise.

## Put impact numbers in context

In a [laboratory study of 23 recreational athletes](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11415629/), vertical ground-reaction force varied with speed and approached about 2.5 times body weight at 6.0 m/s. That is a measurement from a specific experiment. It should not become a fixed `2–3×` value for every speed, surface, and runner, or a complete explanation for injury.

## State the reason for strength work accurately

[WHO guidance](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240014886) recommends muscle-strengthening activity involving major muscle groups on two or more days per week for adults. This is broad health guidance, not a guarantee that one lower-body routine prevents running injury.

In a [randomized pilot of 74 novice runners](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38251299/), the group assigned strengthening plus running did not show a significant advantage in running-related injury or maximal aerobic speed over running alone. The small sample and attrition also mean “strength never helps” would go too far. The accurate line is narrower:

> Strength training is one way to train major muscle groups. It is not personal injury insurance.

## Draft one session

| Movement | Candidates |
|---|---|
| Knee / hip extension | Squat, step-up, light hip hinge |
| Push / pull | Push-up or press, row variation |
| Calf / trunk | Calf raise, a trunk movement you can control |

Choosing one from each row is only a draft. Learn it at the lightest setting and start with an amount that does not disrupt the important runs. A fixed `3 × 10` prescription is not right for every runner.

## Move the day based on the response

| Day | Planning example | What to observe |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Easy run | Fatigue relative to normal |
| Tue | Strength draft | Next-day soreness |
| Thu | Important run | Change in movement or perceived pace |
| Weekend | Long run or rest | Whether weekly fatigue remains |

The table prescribes no distance or duration. If an important run changes because your legs are tired, move strength farther away or reduce it. [NIDDK guidance](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/weight-management/staying-active-at-any-size) not to train the same muscles on consecutive days is another useful boundary.

## Replace the 10% rule with a change log

The [Run Clever randomized trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29527322/) found no clear injury-incidence difference between schedules focused on progressing running intensity and those focused on progressing running volume. That does not support selling `10% per week` or `three build weeks, one down week` as injury-prevention formulas.

Record what changed instead:

- weekly distance
- amount of faster running
- strength load, sets, and reps
- sleep, soreness, pain, and feel on the next run

Changing one input at a time makes the record easier to interpret. It is a troubleshooting method, not a safety guarantee.

Stop the numeric rule when pain recurs or changes your movement. [CDC guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-weight-growth/physical-activity/getting-started.html) recommends building activity gradually and asking a health professional what fits a health condition.
