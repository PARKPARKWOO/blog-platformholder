You open the planner and stop on the first line. Split, exercises, sets, load, and recovery all arrived as one decision.

That experience does not need the explanation “my willpower tank is empty.”

## The fuel-tank model is not settled

In a [preregistered replication across 23 laboratories and 2,141 participants](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27474142/), the ego-depletion effect was small and its 95% confidence interval included zero. Willpower as a single resource that drains with use is not a settled fact.

Planning can still feel heavy. The practical problem—too many options at once—can be reduced without settling a psychology debate.

## Decide in sequence

Fill the plan in this order:

1. Sessions that realistically fit this week
2. Approximate time available for each
3. A layout that does not repeat the same area on consecutive days
4. Exercises worth keeping from the last log
5. One item to change

Count and time do not dictate a split. They remove options that do not fit reality.

## Use two boundaries instead of a fixed split table

[WHO](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240014886) recommends muscle-strengthening activity involving major muscle groups on two or more days per week. [NIDDK](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/weight-management/staying-active-at-any-size) advises against training the same muscles on consecutive days.

Inside those boundaries, test full-body, upper/lower, or chest/back/legs against your schedule. Three weekly sessions do not automatically require a three-way split.

## A log supplies evidence, not an automatic answer

| Observation | Next option |
|---|---|
| Controlled every target rep | Test the smallest available increment |
| Lost control on the last set | Hold or lower the load |
| Repeatedly missed a planned session | Change one of count, day, or length |
| Pain | Stop the numeric rule; modify or get qualified advice |

[ACSM progression guidance](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11828249/) describes percentage progression after a trainee can exceed the target by one or two repetitions and says goals, capacity, and training status matter. It does not justify adding `2.5–5 kg` to every exercise.

## There is no fixed return percentage

One missed week does not automatically require `10%` less load, and two weeks do not automatically require half the exercise list.

Warm up at a light setting and start more conservatively than the pre-break log. Use movement control, breathing, and feel to decide the day. After a long break, illness, or injury, follow [CDC guidance on gradual progression](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-weight-growth/physical-activity/getting-started.html) and consult a qualified professional.

## A weekly check

- How many sessions actually happened last week?
- What fits this week's calendar?
- Does any area repeat on consecutive days?
- Which exercises stay?
- What is the one change?
- Is pain or a health issue part of the decision?

How many minutes this takes is not the point. The useful change is that you are no longer selecting everything from a blank page.
