Ever sent “we're really training this month” to a friend, only to watch the chat go quiet?

The missing piece may not be another person. It may be **operating rules**. “Together” does not define the target, the log, or the boundary around messages.

## What the research can and cannot say

In a [randomized trial of 790 university students](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27617191/), exercise-class attendance was higher in network conditions that included social comparison than in conditions without it. The experiment also included classes, six-person networks, and incentives.

It does not prove that one friend makes every plan last. This guide is an operating template, not an adherence guarantee.

## 1. Agree on boundaries first

One partner or a small group is easier to coordinate. That is a logistics choice, not an efficacy ranking.

Ask three questions before starting:

- Is sharing attendance comfortable?
- When is an unsolicited message welcome?
- How does someone pause or opt out?

## 2. Use four weeks as a review date

Four weeks is not the point at which a habit is complete. It is simply a nearby date for reviewing the setup.

Start with an action rather than an outcome:

- “Record the sessions I actually complete.”
- “Leave one line in the log after training.”

A promise to add `5 kg` brings schedule, recovery, exercise skill, and equipment increments into the score. Even when using a result goal, do not turn two bodies into a direct leaderboard.

## 3. Keep one simple log

| Week | Plan | Actual | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Your plan | Completed value | Work, fatigue, schedule change |

One group chat or shared sheet is enough. If logging feels heavy, remove fields. Do not delete blank weeks. A blank is evidence that the plan and life did not match.

## 4. Keep nudges inside consent

Ask, “Can I check in once on Sunday evening?” Agree on timing and frequency. You can also agree not to ask why a session was missed.

A usable message is short:

> Is this a good time to check the week's log? No need to reply now.

If someone wants to stop, close the record. The relationship comes before the streak.

## 5. Change one item after the review

After four weeks, inspect where the record stopped rather than declaring success or failure.

- Did the planned day repeatedly clash with work?
- Was the log too cumbersome?
- Did the messages help or add pressure?

Change schedule, target, or messaging, one at a time. For load, [ACSM guidance](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11828249/) supports progression after controlled targets while emphasizing individual context. Use the smallest available increment, not one fixed jump for every exercise.

Pain or illness pauses the challenge rule. Follow [CDC guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-weight-growth/physical-activity/getting-started.html) and ask a qualified professional what activity fits the condition. You do not need a replacement exercise to keep the table full.
