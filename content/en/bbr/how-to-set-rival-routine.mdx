Solo "this-month-I'll-train" plans usually last one week.

Take the same plan and frame it as a **"4-week challenge"** with a friend, and it becomes a game. Skipping costs you not just self-disappointment but **letting them down.**

But "let's train together" on its own rarely survives two weeks. Challenges that last have rules. The five steps below run fine on a group chat and a notes app — no app required.

## Why solo plans fail

Solo resolutions collapse for one reason: **nothing happens when you skip.**

The pattern is consistent:

- Week 1: motivated (3–4 sessions)
- Week 2: late nights, social — 1–2 sessions
- Week 3: "I'll skip this week"
- Week 4: "Restart next month"

Willpower alone can't restart the same decision every week. Changing the cost of skipping is far easier.

<Callout type="important" title="What one person changes">
With a friend doing the same challenge, skipping costs shifts from "broke my own promise" to "let my friend down." People are **weaker to others' expectations than their own.**
</Callout>

## Designing the challenge — five steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Step 1 — Pick 1–3 friends at a similar pace">
    Three criteria for who to ask:

    - **Similar training pace** (both beginner, or both intermediate)
    - **Comfortable contact** — someone you can nudge without it being weird
    - **Same gym not required** — what you share is the log, not the location

    Smaller is stronger.

    - 1 person: simplest, sharpest accountability
    - 2–3: more variety, more coordination
    - 5+: not recommended — responsibility diffuses and the effect fades

    Time: 2 min (one message)
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 2 — Agree on one 4-week goal">
    Pick one of two formats, and for the first cycle **choose the count version.**

    **A. Count-based (recommended for beginners)**
    - "**Train 3x/week for 4 weeks**"
    - Easy to measure (showed up = 1)
    - Clear failure threshold (≤2 a week)

    **B. Result-based (intermediate+)**
    - "**Add 5kg to bench press in 4 weeks**"
    - Stronger motivation
    - Vulnerable to injury, sleep, and condition — variables you don't control

    <Callout type="tip">
      Without an established habit, result-based goals fail more often. Switch to them after one successful cycle.
    </Callout>

    Time: 5 min chat
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 3 — Choose one place to log, and only one">
    The most common reason a challenge goes quiet in week three is that **the log lives in two places.**

    Pick one of these and drop the rest.

    - **Group chat** — one line after training. Lightest, but weekly totals are hard to read
    - **Shared spreadsheet** — names × 4 weeks. Easy to total, and it doubles as the retro document
    - **A workout app** — logging and totals in the same place

    Fix the format too. Decide on day one whether you record load/sets/reps or just attendance.

    Time: 3 min
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 4 — Agree on nudge rules in advance">
    A light nudge helps, but **without agreeing when, who, and in what tone**, it becomes nagging.

    Reasonable defaults:

    - **Once a week, on a fixed day** (e.g., Sunday night: "one more this week?")
    - **Mention the miss, don't ask why**
    - **No daily reminders** — the relationship cost outweighs the effect

    <Callout type="warning">
      Prodding sits between fun and gentle pressure. The moment it becomes nagging, the challenge itself becomes a burden.
    </Callout>

    Time: 5 min agreement
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 5 — Run a retro and adjust the next cycle">
    On day 28, put the records side by side and talk for 30 minutes.

    **Both succeed**: meal, drinks, trip — strongest reinforcement

    **One succeeds**: failing side treats — except for injury/health exceptions

    **Both fail**: honest retro → lower the goal next cycle, or change format

    What to look for:

    - The **pattern** behind the best and worst weeks (weekday? overtime? weather?)
    - When exactly the logging stopped
    - The single thing to change next cycle

    Time: 30 min – 1 hour
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Why 4 weeks

Four weeks is **long enough for a habit to start forming and short enough that the finish line stays visible.**

- **1 week is too short** — willpower alone passes it, nothing sticks
- **3 months is too long** — the end feels far, drop-off climbs
- **4 weeks is the sweet spot** — you finish, and you still have energy to start the next one

One successful cycle → raise the goal one notch → three cycles compound into real change. **"Stack 4-week cycles"** is the whole idea.
