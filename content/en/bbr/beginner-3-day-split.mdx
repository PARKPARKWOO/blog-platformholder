Ever stood in front of a machine, touched the handles, and copied the person next to you?

In the first month, **the order of decisions** causes more friction than motivation. If you choose the session, decode the machine, and remember last week's load from scratch every time, the workout feels tiring before it starts.

A 3-day split is one way to write those decisions down. Chest, back, and legs each get a day. Treat it as an editable first draft, not a perfect prescription.

## Set the boundary first

[NIDDK's strength-training guidance](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/weight-management/staying-active-at-any-size) says not to work the same muscles on consecutive days. A schedule such as Monday, Wednesday, and Friday is therefore easy to understand.

The calendar cannot decide recovery by itself. Add time when soreness or whole-body fatigue remains, or when an old injury is relevant. Sharp, recurring, or daily-life-limiting pain belongs outside the spreadsheet and with a qualified professional.

## A four-week draft

| Day | Focus | How to choose movements |
|---|---|---|
| A | Chest | One multi-joint press plus an accessory you can control |
| B | Back | One multi-joint pull plus an accessory you can control |
| C | Legs | One squat or leg-press pattern plus a hamstring or calf movement |

Exercise count is not the goal. If time is short or technique changes, remove the later accessory first. [ACSM's progression position stand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11828249/) discusses larger-muscle and multi-joint work before smaller, single-joint work, while also saying the guidance must be interpreted through the person's goals, capacity, and training status.

## Do not guess at machine setup

Machines differ in pivots, safety systems, and adjustment ranges. A seat number that works on one machine does not transfer to another.

1. Read the diagram and warnings on the machine.
2. Confirm that adjustment pins and safety stops are locked.
3. Test a comfortable range at the lightest setting.
4. Ask staff or a qualified coach when the setup is unclear.

Moving without pain does not prove that the setup is perfect. It is still a better check than selecting the working load first.

## Four log fields are enough

| Date | Exercise | Load · sets · reps | Technique / comfort note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Example | Chest press | Your completed values | Stable / uncomfortable / painful |

The example is a format, not a target. Record what you actually did, plus a short setup or comfort note. The next session starts from that evidence rather than memory.

When all target repetitions stay controlled, you may test the smallest available increment. Hold or lower the load if position changes or targets are missed. A fixed `2.5–5 kg` rule cannot account for different dumbbells, cables, machines, and trainees.

## What to review after four weeks

- Were the planned days realistic?
- Did fatigue in one area last into its next session?
- Did the setup note change every time?
- Did technique stay ahead of load?

Change one item and observe again. Changing the schedule, exercises, and load together makes the useful change hard to identify.
