Rebuilding your resume and interview notes from scratch for every role can create duplicated work.

Using the same generic answers without reading the posting can obscure the connection between your experience and this role. One option is **a repeatable process for arranging existing evidence around the current posting**.

<Callout type="important" title="Employer instructions come first">
The counts, answer lengths, and review intervals in this article are editing heuristics. If the employer publishes instructions for resume length, file type, interview format, or evaluation criteria, follow those instructions first.
</Callout>

## What you can verify and what you would have to guess

Applicants can directly verify materials such as:

- duties and qualifications in the posting
- submission instructions in the application form
- process information on the employer's official careers site
- experience you can state truthfully in your resume

You may not know how interviewers design questions, how an automated application tool processes a document, or how one phrase affects selection. Those details can differ by company or remain unpublished. Do not turn them into universal rules.

The preparation boundary is therefore simple: **follow published instructions, compare the role with your evidence, and do not pretend to know the internal process.**

## Turn one posting into a preparation table

Copy the main duties and qualifications into a two-column table.

| Posting text | My evidence or preparation note |
|---|---|
| "Experience designing APIs under high traffic" | Scale, my decision, and result from a payments API |
| "Event-driven processing experience" | No direct experience; similar problem and learning plan |
| "Comfort in a testing culture" | Why I introduced integration tests and what changed |

This table does not predict the interview. It separates rows where you have evidence from rows that need more explanation.

For a row with evidence, add four fields:

1. Situation — when and under what constraints
2. Action — a decision you actually made
3. Result — measurement and method, if one exists
4. Link — the part of this posting it relates to

For a blank row, mark `no direct experience`, `transferable evidence`, or `learning plan`. Do not disguise a missing experience with the posting's vocabulary.

## Do not overestimate keywords or ATS rules

The purpose of this worksheet is not to collect a keyword score. It is to **place your evidence beside the employer's published work for comparison**.

Applicant-tracking behavior can differ by product, employer, and configuration. This article therefore makes no universal claim such as “this word gets you through” or “this layout gets you rejected.” Any format or writing instruction published by the employer comes first.

## What to prepare before a posting appears

### Experience cards

For each resume experience, write situation, action, result, and lesson. Start with a few cards. Accuracy matters more than card count.

### Official careers-page list

Keep company, role, official careers-page link, and last-checked date in one sheet. Choose a checking interval based on active deadlines and your schedule; there is no requirement to check every day.

### Application-file log

Record the date, company, role, and submitted filename. Keep factual experience bullets stable, while separating the summary and ordering that may change by posting.

## A short review after you find a posting

1. Read the employer's instructions first.
2. Copy the main duties and qualifications into the table.
3. Attach relevant experience cards.
4. Add an acknowledgement and plan for blank rows.
5. Re-check that every use of posting language matches work you actually did.

The time required depends on the posting and how well your experience is already organized. This process does not promise a preparation time; it avoids rewriting the same facts from zero.

## Closing

Tailoring to a role-specific posting does not mean presenting yourself as a different person at every company. It means connecting the same evidence accurately to the work the employer published.

Use the employer's instructions and posting, not guesses about hiring customs or automation. The detailed worksheet continues in [the five-step method for building practice-question candidates from a posting and resume](/en/blog/mirror-view/create-interview-questions).
