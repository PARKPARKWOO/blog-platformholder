A few years ago, periodic mass hiring announcements were the anchor of job preparation. You prepared against the spring and autumn cycles and that was the calendar.

It reads differently now. Browse a job board and role-based rolling postings and internship-to-hire tracks are noticeably more visible than periodic mass hiring. Anecdotally, prep has shifted toward "waiting for a posting that could appear any week."

This isn't just a shift in *when* you apply. **The grammar of interview prep itself changes.**

## Mass-hiring era — "evaluate the common denominator"

**Questions standardized because of the scale of evaluation.** When hundreds or thousands of candidates have to be compared on one scale, the questions have to be identical for fairness to hold.

So the same items repeated:

- "Tell me about your strength"
- "Why our company"
- "Where do you see yourself in 10 years"

Sample answers piled up in books, blogs, and videos, and polishing those answers was a large share of prep. With the questions fixed, reusing answers was the rational strategy.

## Rolling-hiring era — "verify fit against the posting"

**In rolling hiring the requirements for the seat already exist as a written document, so the interviewer builds questions from it.**

Roles, teams, and required skills are defined, and one or two people are hired. The interviewer's questions converge to one:

> **"Does this person fit *this posting* precisely?"**

<Callout type="important" title="How questions change">
**Mass-hiring style (generic answer works)**
> "Tell me about a recent collaboration."

**Rolling-hiring style (posting-based)**
> "The posting lists B2B SaaS data pipelines and your resume mentions them. Which tools, and what was the hardest part?"
</Callout>

The second question was crafted by an interviewer who **read your resume and matched it to the posting beforehand**. No generic answer set survives it.

## Automated first-round screening — what's certain and what isn't

**One caveat is worth stating plainly. You'll often hear that more companies use automated tools in resume and first-round screening, but the actual scoring methods differ by company and aren't published.**

So claims of the form "the AI scores this factor this way" are mostly speculation. This post won't ask you to prepare against that speculation.

Only one thing is certain: **whether a human or a tool reads it, the document that defines the criteria is the posting.** Speaking well about something the posting never mentions is weaker than speaking about a posting item with evidence attached — under any screening method.

## Three shifts in how to prepare

### 1. Replace "100 generic questions" with "*my* resume × *this* posting"

**Even within the same role, companies emphasize different requirements.**

- Company A backend: large-scale traffic
- Company B backend: domain modeling, DDD
- Company C backend: AI infra ops

All three say "backend engineer," yet the interview questions are completely different. One generic set covering all three means preparing about a third of the depth each one needs.

### 2. Get the posting's vocabulary into your answers

**Quoting one phrase from the posting structurally blocks answer reuse.**

> "The ○○ point in the posting overlaps exactly with the ○○ work I led for the past six months."

Change the company and the phrase you quote changes, so the answer has to change with it. To the person listening, it also reads as "this candidate actually read us."

Quote only within the range of work you've genuinely done. Borrowing terminology for something you haven't done surfaces in the very next question.

### 3. Acknowledge the gap and show a plan

**Rolling hiring is closer to looking for someone who knows how to close a gap than someone with no gaps**, because there's one seat and the work in it is specific.

> "○○ is still my weak area, but I've handled a similar problem with ○○, and I plan to get through ○○ before I start."

One line of acknowledgement plus one line of plan is enough — and considerably safer than talking around a gap.

## So what to do today — start from one posting

**The key is changing the unit of preparation from 'season' to 'posting'.**

Open one posting you plan to apply to and copy 5–10 lines of its requirements and responsibilities verbatim — don't summarize. Next to each line, paste the sentence from your resume that supports it. Lines with evidence become depth questions; empty lines become gap questions.

That single table is your company-specific question set. The full procedure is written up as five steps in [how to generate your own interview questions from a job posting + your resume](/en/blog/mirror-view/create-interview-questions).

## Wrap-up

For employers, the reason usually cited for rolling hiring is filling only the seats that are needed, when they're needed. A return to mass-hiring volumes doesn't look likely.

Prep has to follow. **From "memorize the common denominator" to "*my* resume × *this* posting."** What changes isn't how much you prepare — it's the unit you prepare in.
