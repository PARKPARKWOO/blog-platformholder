Sometimes the company name changes and the answer still works word for word.

That may reflect the material you practiced, not a lack of effort. This article does not claim to know how interviewers write questions. It checks one narrower thing: **whether your answer contains a sentence linked to this posting**.

<Callout type="important" title="This is an editing check, not a prediction">
Questions and evaluation methods vary by employer, stage, and interviewer. Follow any interview format or preparation instruction the employer publishes.
</Callout>

## Four fields for separating generic and posting-specific material

These fields are not a universal formula for a strong answer. They are an editing heuristic for finding missing information.

1. Posting context: a duty or qualification the employer actually published
2. Your evidence: work and decisions you actually handled
3. The link: why that evidence relates to this role
4. The gap: an acknowledgement and plan where direct experience is missing

With only the second field, you can describe an experience but leave unclear why you chose it for this role. The third field joins the posting to your evidence in one sentence.

## Same question, different edit

**Generic answer**
> "Your company is growing quickly and the values fit mine. I want to grow in this environment."

**Answer with a link**
> "The ○○ duty emphasized in the posting relates to the ○○ work I owned on my last project. My decision was ○○, and I verified the result through ○○."

The second answer combines a published duty with your action. It still must not connect to work you did not do or guess at an unpublished team condition.

## A transcript test for repeated answers

Say the answer out loud and turn it into text. Mark three things:

1. sentences that still work after deleting the company and product names
2. abstract words repeated across several answers
3. sections where setup is long but action, result, or link is short

If you want a length constraint for comparing versions, choose the same interval yourself. It is an editing condition, not a known employer preference or scoring rule.

Not every sentence needs to be company-specific. The facts of your experience should repeat. What changes is **the sentence connecting that evidence to this posting**.

## Ways to turn a spoken answer into text

### Manual transcription

If you are not transcribing from a recording, label the text as a reconstructed note rather than an exact transcript.

### Phone voice memo

A recording can be replayed for transcription. Check storage and backup settings when the answer contains sensitive work details.

### Browser speech recognition

If a speech-recognition tool supports your browser and language, it can produce a draft transcript. Model downloads and storage or transmission behavior vary, so check them before use. Treat the output as transcription to verify, not an evaluation.

## Patterns for a link sentence

### Pattern: quote the posting

> "The posting's line about 'designing APIs under high traffic' relates to the ○○ work I handled on a payments API."

Use it only where the wording accurately fits your experience.

### Pattern: compare a similar problem

> "I cannot assume this team has the same condition, but I met a similar ○○ problem during an early project phase and handled it with ○○."

Keep the employer's situation as something to verify, not a fact.

### Pattern: form a question about the first check

> "My ○○ experience appears related to this duty. I would first want to understand how the team currently decides ○○."

This avoids promising an action before you know the context.

### Rules for every pattern

- Use posting language only where it matches work you actually did.
- Start with one link sentence per answer.
- Do not guess at internal conditions, question design, or selection outcomes.

## Freeze the experience card; review the link

Build an experience card with situation, action, and result. Those facts should not change when the company changes.

| Keep fixed | Review for each posting |
|---|---|
| situation, your action, verifiable result | which published duty it relates to |
| measured figures and the method | whether the posting term accurately describes the work |
| lesson from the experience | how to acknowledge a gap |

There is no correct number of cards or minutes per card. Start with a few experiences you use often, then adjust to the employer's instructions and posting.

## Closing

Repeated answers do not require invented experiences. Connect the same evidence accurately to the work published in this posting.
