At month end, settlement exports collected in one sheet may disagree with a figure recorded earlier. If the formula does not explain it, inspect whether **sale-date estimates, final values, file definitions, and edit history** have been mixed.

This article has no frequency data for these scenarios. It also does not assert any channel's file format or policy. It covers operational risks and a manual sheet structure for reconciling several exports.

<Callout type="note" title="Check these first">
- current official settlement and export guidance for the channels you use
- a status field showing whether a sale-date value is estimated or final
- separation between raw exports and transformed sheets
- a record of the value before each correction
</Callout>

## Risk 1 — estimates and final values share one field

A value recorded on the sale date may change in the later settlement. Depending on the transaction, fees, outbound or return shipping, cancellations, returns, or promotion contributions may be reflected at a different time.

Channels and transactions do not all follow one sequence. Keep `estimated`, `final`, and `adjusted` states separate, and use the channel's official settlement record as the source for final status.

## Risk 2 — identical labels are treated as identical amounts

One file's `sales amount` may be gross before fees while another's may be an expected payout. Tax, shipping, and cancellation treatment may differ too.

Build a mapping table when you first receive a file.

| Check | File A | File B |
|---|---|---|
| amount definition | gross / expected / final | gross / expected / final |
| fees | separate / included | separate / included |
| shipping | separate / included | separate / included |
| cancellation or return | same file / separate file | same file / separate file |
| date basis | sale date / settlement date | sale date / settlement date |

Do not guess. Check current official guidance and the notes in the actual export.

## Risk 3 — product titles become join keys

Color, size, condition, and included-item labels can vary between files for the same item. An exact-string lookup can miss those differences.

One manual alternative is a seller-controlled identifier with channel-specific titles stored as aliases. Which dimensions belong in the identifier depends on the operation. Start with the dimensions that must be settled separately; keep the rest as reference fields.

## Risk 4 — old values are overwritten

Typing a final payout over an estimate or replacing a stock count removes the earlier value. A later discrepancy is harder to trace without knowing what changed and when.

Preserve the original export. In the transformed sheet, append an `adjustment` row with the original row ID, adjustment date, and reason instead of deleting the old row.

## Risk 5 — cost assumptions exist only inside formulas

When fee rates, shipping, or return allowances exist only as formula constants, it is hard to inspect which assumptions produced an earlier result.

Move assumptions to a separate sheet with name, value, effective date, and source. Add a new row when a value changes. This is a recordkeeping method, not a promise of profit accuracy.

## A manual spreadsheet structure

<Steps>
<Step title="Record one event per row">
Give purchase, intake, sale, final settlement, return, and adjustment separate rows instead of repeatedly changing an item row.
</Step>
<Step title="Separate raw and normalized files">
Do not edit the channel export. Match column names and definitions in another sheet.
</Step>
<Step title="Keep an internal identifier and the source ID">
Join products on your internal identifier while preserving channel order and settlement IDs for comparison.
</Step>
<Step title="Attach an effective date and source to assumptions">
For fees and shipping, record when you checked the official guidance and its link or document name.
</Step>
<Step title="Separate estimated, final, and adjusted states">
Do not combine mid-period estimates and official final settlements into the same total.
</Step>
</Steps>

This structure cannot guarantee that differences disappear. It preserves material for comparing the file, assumption, and adjustment where a difference appeared.

## Frequently asked questions

**What should I align first when merging settlement files?**
Align amount and date definitions before column names. Keep a mapping table based on current official guidance and the actual exports.

**Can I keep using a spreadsheet?**
It may remain practical when volume and formats are simple and you can preserve raw records, definitions, and edit history. There is no fixed channel or file-count threshold.

**Is sale-date margin final?**
Later costs or adjustments may apply depending on the transaction. Mark it as an estimate and reconcile it with the official settlement record.
