If two orders arrive close together while one physical unit is listed on several channels, a seller may be unable to fulfill both.

This article does not claim how often that occurs, what penalty a particular channel applies, or how multi-channel listing affects visibility. It covers one narrower task: **tracking how many sales commitments sit against one physical unit**.

<Callout type="note" title="Scope of this article">
- no frequency or sales-performance estimate
- no interpretation of a channel's permission, prohibition, or enforcement
- current official seller policies must be checked before acting
- a manual stock-sheet workaround you can use today
</Callout>

## Risk 1 — the sheet stores physical quantity but not commitments

A stock sheet may show `1` while listings remain open on several channels. If the sheet contains only physical quantity, it cannot show where the item is listed or whether an order has already committed it.

Separate these fields per item.

| Field | What to record |
|---|---|
| physical quantity | quantity you own and have checked |
| available quantity | quantity your internal policy allows for a new order |
| committed quantity | quantity already assigned to a confirmed sale or other commitment |
| listing channels | channels whose current listing status you checked |
| last checked | time you reconciled the channel view with the sheet |

## Risk 2 — manual updates take several steps

In a manual workflow, you see a sale, open another channel, find the item, and update its listing status in sequence. Another order is an operational possibility during that work interval.

Display-update time, order-confirmation conditions, and cancellation outcomes may differ by channel. Do not state that one channel is slower or that repeated cancellations reduce visibility without current official evidence.

## Risk 3 — owned quantity is confused with available quantity

An item may be owned or visible in a system while your policy keeps it unavailable for a new order.

- sold but not yet shipped
- return or inspection hold
- transfer to another location or channel warehouse
- purchased but not yet received and checked

The exact states subtracted from available quantity must follow official channel definitions and your own documented policy. This list is a review example.

## Risk 4 — cancellation work is not recorded

An unfulfilled order can create operational work: updating listings, communicating with a buyer, reconciling source records, and adjusting the stock sheet. Specific fees, ratings, or selling-rights effects depend on channel policy and are not asserted here.

Record date, item ID, channel, reason, and work performed. The log is for examining your own operation, not measuring industry frequency or policy consequences.

## Risk 5 — there is no exclusive or shared rule

Applying the same listing method to every item makes it hard to distinguish a single-unit item from one with spare quantity.

Two internal states can serve as a starting point:

- `exclusive` — listed on one channel at a time
- `shared` — listed on several channels while available and committed quantity are checked

Making a single-unit item `exclusive` is a conservative option. Check each channel's current official policy for whether multiple listings are allowed and what counts as a confirmed order.

## Fields you can add today

<Steps>
<Step title="Separate available quantity">
Start with `available = received and checked − committed − internal holds`. Document the state definitions for your operation.
</Step>
<Step title="Assign exclusive or shared status">
Set an internal listing rule per item. You can start single-unit items as exclusive and review exceptions manually.
</Step>
<Step title="Record listing state and last-checked time">
Keep a row for where the item is listed and when you last checked it. Re-check the actual channel state instead of trusting the sheet alone.
</Step>
<Step title="Write the update sequence">
Base the sequence on official procedures and your actual workflow. Do not invent rankings about visibility or platform update speed.
</Step>
<Step title="Log cancellations and adjustments with reason codes">
Use internal codes such as `duplicate-commitment`, `stock-mismatch`, `price-error`, or `shipping-issue`, then record the work performed. Review patterns only in your own data.
</Step>
</Steps>

This sheet cannot guarantee that a duplicate sale is prevented. It is a temporary way to bring manual-decision states and check times into one place.

## Frequently asked questions

**Should a single-unit item be listed on only one channel?**
This article offers `exclusive` as a conservative internal default, not a universal rule. Check the current official policy of each channel before multi-listing.

**Why is available quantity different from physical quantity?**
It avoids promising a unit already committed to another order or held for inspection or transfer. Define the held states for your operation.

**Which channel listing should I update first?**
There is no universal channel ranking here. Use official procedures and your actual workflow, then directly confirm the current state on each channel after a sale.
