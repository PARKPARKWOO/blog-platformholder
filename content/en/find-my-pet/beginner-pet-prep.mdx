The day you bring a new pet home, it's impossible to look away.

First walk, first treat, first time they curl up and fall asleep — the happy moments stack up fast. Thinking about "what if" in the middle of all that is genuinely hard.

That's exactly why this guide exists. **Four things to check off within the first one to four weeks.** Each takes five minutes or less today.

## Why prepare now?

Because the payoff is measured in **minutes saved right after a loss**.

Pets go missing for reasons that have nothing to do with being a careless owner. A worn leash clip. A door left open for ten seconds. A sudden loud noise on a walk. Ordinary moments, more common than most people expect.

The hard part is that when it happens, **every second spent in a panic is a second not spent searching**. Choosing photos, signing in, creating a chat room for tips — each costs minutes right when you have none. Doing them in advance removes them entirely.

<Callout type="note" title="The golden window">
  In the first one to two hours after a pet disappears, they tend to stay close — often hiding within a short radius of where they were last seen.
  Getting the word out to your neighborhood inside that window is what changes outcomes.
  Preparation is what buys you the time.
</Callout>

## The 4-step checklist

<Steps>
  <Step title="Step 1 — Attach an ID tag on day one">
    An ID tag only works if it's already on when something goes wrong.

    "I'll order one this weekend" is how the gap appears. A pet can slip out before the tag arrives.

    What to put on the tag:
    - Your pet's name
    - Your mobile number

    You don't need a home address. A phone number is enough for anyone who finds them to reach you immediately.

    <Callout type="tip" title="Choosing a tag">
      Stainless steel holds up far better than plastic over time.
      Engraved text outlasts printed or sticker-style tags — rain, scratches, and daily wear eventually fade the print.
    </Callout>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 2 — Microchip and registry verification">
    A microchip is a rice-grain-sized identification device implanted under the skin. Any vet can do the procedure in a few minutes.

    **The chip alone is not enough. What matters is the registry entry.**

    A shelter that scans a chip can only reunite you with your pet if your current contact details are correctly listed. If you move, change your phone number, or adopt the pet from someone else, the registry needs updating.

    In Korea, the official system is [animal.go.kr](https://www.animal.go.kr) — the government-run national animal protection database. After the procedure, confirm your record there directly.

    Post-procedure checklist:
    - [ ] Receive the 15-digit chip number and keep it somewhere safe
    - [ ] Look the chip number up on animal.go.kr and verify your contact details
    - [ ] Update the registry whenever your phone number or address changes

    <Callout type="note">
      animal.go.kr is a Korean government public service. Find-My-Pet is not an official partner of that system — it simply displays the openly published public data. Microchip registration must be completed directly through animal.go.kr.
    </Callout>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 3 — Build a monthly photo archive">
    The first thing anyone asks for when a pet goes missing is a recent photo.

    In the panic of the moment, scrolling through your camera roll often turns up photos from six months ago — blurry, backlit, or with the face half cut off.

    **Once a month**, take three shots:

    | Angle | Why it matters |
    |-------|----------------|
    | Front-facing (eyes, nose, mouth) | Helps people identify the face |
    | Full side-body | Shows overall build and coat colour |
    | Distinctive markings or scars | Distinguishes your pet from lookalikes |

    Store them in cloud storage — Google Photos, iCloud, or similar. If your phone is lost or dead in an emergency, you can pull them from any other device.

    A missing post accepts **up to 3 images in jpg, jpeg, png, or gif**, so deciding in advance which three you'd use makes the moment faster.
  </Step>
