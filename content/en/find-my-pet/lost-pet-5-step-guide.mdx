When a pet disappears, several jobs arrive at once. The first task is not to guess a search radius. It is to protect the **last-seen point and one shared set of facts**.

## 1. Secure the last-seen point and the facts

Keep one person at the last-seen point when possible. Give every helper the same four items:

- a clear, current photo
- the last-seen time
- an exact map location
- direction of travel and identifying details such as collar, clothing, or tag

Near traffic, construction, or another hazard, stay in a safe position and contact the responsible authority.

## 2. Check nearby while publishing the missing information

In one household survey, recovered dogs were often found through active neighborhood searching. A separate missing-cat study found physical searching important, but it did not establish one radius for every cat. Cats' previous outdoor access was associated with different found distances, and the study was a retrospective, self-selected survey.

The [US household study of lost-dog and lost-cat recovery methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4494319/) and the [missing-cat search and location study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5789300/) are useful because they separate species and circumstances.

While some people check the area, another person should publish the missing information. If you are alone, publish first and then move without looking at your phone while walking.

Read [how to divide the first hour](/en/blog/find-my-pet/why-first-hour-matters) and [how to expand a search without a universal radius](/en/blog/find-my-pet/how-far-do-lost-pets-travel) for the focused versions.

## 3. Start official reporting and shelter-notice checks

[Article 39 of Korea's current Animal Protection Act](https://www.law.go.kr/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1028318771) says a person who finds a lost or abandoned animal may report it to the local government or an animal shelter. Korea's Ministry of Agriculture also provides [online reporting through the national system and the 1577-0954 reporting line](https://mafra.go.kr/bbs/home/792/577317/artclView.do).

Check shelter notices at the same time. Include adjacent districts instead of treating an administrative border as a search boundary. The [regional shelter-notice guide](/en/blog/find-my-pet/check-shelter-notices-by-region) explains the notice period and contact sequence.

## 4. Distribute one source of truth by link and flyer

Retyping the details for every channel invites mismatched times and locations. Share one post link and edit that post first when something changes.

[Create a missing post on Find-My-Pet](https://findmypet.platformholder.site/register?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=fmp-response-2026q3&utm_content=pillar-register) to keep photos, loss time, a map location, and contact information together. The public detail page can be shared, and sighting reports collect under the same post.

For offline distribution, use the guide to [build a flyer with identifying details and a safer contact choice](/en/blog/find-my-pet/how-to-write-a-flyer).

## 5. Update the search from sighting evidence

Confirm three things for every report:

1. sighting time
2. exact location
3. direction of travel

Prefer a recent, specific sighting over an older estimate. Find-My-Pet's Core, Likely, and Possible areas are reference estimates for deciding where to start, not lines that rule other places out.

If you are the person who found a loose animal, follow the [safe observation and official reporting sequence](/en/blog/find-my-pet/saw-a-stray-what-to-do).

## Prepare separately before a loss or a trip

Response and prevention have different jobs. New owners can prepare [an ID tag, registration details, current photos, and service access](/en/blog/find-my-pet/beginner-pet-prep). Before visitors or travel, use the [holiday checklist for doors, transport, and identification](/en/blog/find-my-pet/holiday-season-pet-safety).

After reunion, change the post to `FOUND` from your own-post list on the profile page and update the places where you shared it.
