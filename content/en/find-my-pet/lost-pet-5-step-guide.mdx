The first night after a pet goes missing is the longest.

Everyone tells you "the first hour matters" — but in that moment, it's hard to remember exactly what to do.

This guide compresses that hour into five steps, and notes the exact screen to use in [Find-My-Pet](https://findmypet.platformholder.site?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=fmp-launch-2026q2&utm_content=howto-intro) at each one.

<Callout type="note" title="There is no app to install">
  Find-My-Pet is a web service. Nothing to download from a store — open `findmypet.platformholder.site` in your phone's browser and you're in.
  Browsing and reading posts work without an account; a Kakao sign-in is only needed to publish your own post.
</Callout>

## Why the first hour is decisive

Pets unfamiliar with the outdoors usually **hide nearby instead of running far** in the minutes after they vanish. Under parked cars, narrow alleys, beneath stairs, utility nooks — tight, dark spaces.

So in this window, thoroughly covering **500 m–1 km around the last known point** beats widening the search area.

After that, two things get worse at once: the animal's range grows, and posts you made in community feeds sink down where fewer new people see them.

<Callout type="important" title="Two levers you control">
  Speed and channel diversity decide what happens in the golden window.
  Register once, quickly — then push that single link into as many channels as you can at the same time.
</Callout>

## The 5-step flow used in this guide

These five steps are **this article's own sequence**. Find-My-Pet also has its own [step-by-step lost pet guide](https://findmypet.platformholder.site/guide?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=fmp-launch-2026q2&utm_content=howto-guide-link) page, organised by topic instead: local search, flyers, police and emergency services, social channels, the national animal protection system, and shelter checks. The ordering differs, so skim both and follow whichever fits your situation.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Step 1 — Search a 500m–1km radius on foot">
    Start at the disappearance point. Walk usual routes: walking paths, playgrounds, under stairs, beneath parked cars.

    Name calls, treat-bag sounds, and familiar toy noises work best. Bring family or friends and split into **different directions at the same time** — three paths once each covers far more than one path three times.

    If you're looking for a cat, don't widen the radius. Re-check the same nearby spots during quiet hours instead.

    <Callout type="warning">
      Stay calm. Loud noises and running steps can push an already-frightened pet further away.
    </Callout>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 2 — File a missing post on Find-My-Pet">
    Instead of retyping the same details into every group chat and community app, [file one post](https://findmypet.platformholder.site?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=fmp-launch-2026q2&utm_content=howto-step2) and it lands on the public list, the Kakao map, your own dashboard, and the **combined search** that covers missing pets and shelter listings together.

    What the form asks for:
    - Up to 3 photos (jpg, jpeg, png, gif)
    - Time of loss
    - Location — type an address and the map fills in automatically, then tap the map to correct the exact spot
    - Species and breed (a fixed list: 30 dog breeds, 15 cat breeds, 5 other)
    - Sex and a description
    - Contact — add a Kakao OpenChat URL to take tips without exposing your phone number
    - Reward (optional, fine to leave empty)

    Leave the status on the default **SEARCHING**. The form does let you pick SEEN at creation time, but that's for reporting an animal you spotted, not one you lost.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 3 — Use the search-radius map to choose your next direction">
    Once the post is up, its detail page shows a **reachable-area map**. Based on the time elapsed and the species, it estimates how far the animal could have gone in three bands: Core, Likely, and Possible.

    - **Dogs**: estimated along the actual walking road network
    - **Cats and other animals**: shown as a circular estimate

    As hours pass, the banner text above the map and the map's range shift with it, moving you to guidance that fits that stage.

    <Callout type="note">
      This is a **reference estimate**, nothing more. Real movement depends on terrain, roads, and weather. Never use it to rule an area out.
    </Callout>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 4 — Call vets, police, and shelters while the link spreads">
    Run the phone calls and the link sharing in parallel.

    **Calls and visits**
    - Local police non-emergency line
    - City/county animal protection office
    - Veterinary clinics within 5 km — send a photo, ask to be called if the pet turns up
    - [animal.go.kr](https://www.animal.go.kr), Korea's national animal protection system — confirm your microchip contact details are current

    **Sharing**

    The post's share buttons cover KakaoTalk, your device's native share sheet, plain link copy, and a pre-written blurb for local-life apps. Recipients need no account to see the post, map, and contact details.

    - KakaoTalk group chats and neighborhood OpenChat
    - Instagram Stories with a link sticker
    - Naver neighborhood cafes and parent communities
    - Local-life apps (e.g. Karrot)

    <Callout type="tip">
      **One line + one photo + the post link** is the format that travels fastest. Short information outruns a long story.
    </Callout>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 5 — Work the sightings, then close the post">
    Any signed-in user can file a **"I saw them here" report** on your post, and each one triggers an in-app notification to you. Notifications collect under the bell icon (refreshed every 30 seconds), with the full history on the notifications page.

    When a report arrives:
    1. Open the notification and read the reported spot and time
    2. Go check it in person
    3. Edit your post body to record the newly known sighting time and location

    As reports stack up under the post with their map, the direction of travel becomes readable.

    Once you have your pet back, go to your **post list on the profile page (`/profile`)** and flip the post to **FOUND**. That stops duplicate tips, and anyone who bookmarked the post gets notified that the status changed.

    <Callout type="warning" title="The status toggle has exactly two states">
      Status moves between `SEARCHING` and `FOUND` only.
      There is no way to switch a published post to `SEEN`, and pressing the toggle on a post created as SEEN sends it to SEARCHING with no way back.
    </Callout>
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Worth doing in parallel

**Check shelter listings too**

The [Find-My-Pet home](https://findmypet.platformholder.site?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=fmp-launch-2026q2&utm_content=howto-mid-cta) shows missing posts and shelter listings together by default. The chips at the top let you narrow down: **`가족을 기다려요` (Waiting for family)** shows only shelter listings sourced from the government animal protection system, and `집을 잃었어요` (Lost their home) shows only missing posts.

Someone may already have taken your pet to a shelter, so work through province and district filters alongside your physical search. You can also subscribe to a region and get notified when new listings appear there.

**Paper flyers**

Digital reach misses the neighbors who walk the same route every morning. A sheet of paper doesn't.

On your own missing post, a **"전단지 QR" (Flyer QR)** button appears — visible only to you as the post author. It prints an A4 flyer from one of three templates, lets you edit the Korean copy, and embeds a QR code pointing at your post, so the paper stays current without a reprint. The same screen records where you posted each flyer on a map and tracks whether you've collected it.

Full walkthrough: [How to make a lost pet flyer](/en/blog/find-my-pet/how-to-write-a-flyer).

<Callout type="note" title="Privacy">
  Don't put your home address or vehicle plate in the post.
  Use a Kakao OpenChat link as your contact and handle follow-ups 1:1.
</Callout>
