A flyer can be the single tip that brings your pet home.

It's especially the channel that reaches **neighbors who don't use apps or community feeds** — for them, a sheet of paper at eye level works where digital reach simply stops.

But a badly made flyer gets walked past. Here's how to make one that gets read, put it up legally, and keep track of what you posted — in five steps.

## Why flyers still matter

Because digital reach only touches **people already using that channel**.

When you [file a missing post on Find-My-Pet](https://findmypet.platformholder.site?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=fmp-launch-2026q2&utm_content=flyer-intro), it appears on the public list, the Kakao map, and the combined search immediately, and one link carries it into group chats. But the people most likely to actually run into a lost pet are:

- Older neighbors walking the same route at the same hour
- Students on school routes
- Street cleaners
- Delivery riders

Many of them will never open a missing-pet listing. **A piece of paper at eye level reaches them.**

<Callout type="tip" title="The QR code is what joins paper to digital">
A flyer's weakness is that it freezes at the moment it was printed.
Putting your post link on it as a QR code removes that weakness — change the post, and what a scanner sees changes with it, no reprint needed.
</Callout>

## Five steps to make and distribute

<Steps>
  <Step title="Step 1 — File the missing post first">
    The post comes before the flyer, because the QR code needs somewhere to point.

    Sign in to [Find-My-Pet](https://findmypet.platformholder.site?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=fmp-launch-2026q2&utm_content=flyer-step1) with Kakao and add up to 3 photos, the time of loss, the location (type an address, then tap the map to correct the spot), and a contact. Using a Kakao OpenChat URL as the contact means you don't have to print your phone number in large type.

    Time: 5 min
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 2 — Pick one photo and five pieces of information">
    Don't use several photos. Pick **the single most identifiable one**.

    Good photo:
    - Within the last 3 months
    - Clear front face
    - Natural light
    - Distinctive features visible (spots, coat pattern, tail colour)

    Skip:
    - Blurry shots
    - Too dark or too bright
    - Outfits that hide markings
    - Side-only angles

    Then limit the text to five items:

    1. **Headline**: "Lost dog" / "Missing cat" — 40pt or larger
    2. **Name and breed**: "Dubu / Shiba mix, 7kg, fawn"
    3. **Date and place**: "May 1, 2026 — near OO Park"
    4. **Contact**: 24pt or larger, or a QR/link to an OpenChat room
    5. **(Optional) reward**: omit if it feels uncomfortable

    <Callout type="tip">
      The more text, the less gets read. **Headline + photo + contact** must land within 3 seconds.
    </Callout>

    Time: 3 min
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 3 — Build the flyer">
    Two routes.

    **Option A — Find-My-Pet's flyer print view (fast)**

    Open your own missing post's detail page and a `전단지 QR` (Flyer QR) button appears — **visible only to you as the author**. From there you can produce an A4 flyer directly.

    - Choose one of three templates: **URGENT** (high-alert), **WARM** (softer tone), **MINIMAL** (plain)
    - The Korean copy is editable, so you can rewrite the lines
    - **The QR code points at your post**, so the printed sheet stays current as you update it
    - Print straight to A4

    **Option B — free design tools (more control)**

    If you want to control the layout yourself, use a free template on Canva or Mirikanvas.

    - **Canva** (https://canva.com): "lost pet poster"
    - **Mirikanvas** (https://www.miricanvas.com): Korean-friendly templates

    Either way, generate a QR code for your post link and place it in a corner.

    Time: 5 min for option A, 10–15 min for option B
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 4 — Print, and post only where it's allowed">
    Local print shop or 24h self-service kiosk: **50–100 copies** (in Korea, roughly 100 KRW per A4 sheet in black and white, 200–300 KRW in colour).

    **Posting priority (1–2 km around the loss point)**

    1. Vet clinic boards
    2. Trail and park entrance boards
    3. Convenience stores, cafes, salons (ask the owner first)
    4. Apartment elevators (with management permission)
    5. Official bus stop boards
    6. Official municipal community boards

    <Callout type="warning">
      Posting on utility poles, traffic signs, or street trees can incur fines. Use official boards, or get permission first.
    </Callout>

    Slipping each sheet into a clear file or zip bag buys you several extra days of rain.

    Time: 1–2 hours including travel
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 5 — Record what you posted, and share the same image">
    **Don't rely on memory for where the sheets went.** The flyer management panel on your post lets you pin each posting spot on a map along with how many sheets you left there, and tick the ones you later **collect**, so what's still out there stays visible at a glance. Like the print view, this panel is visible only to you as the post's author.

    Then run the same image through digital channels:

    - **KakaoTalk groups and neighborhood OpenChat**: image + post link
    - **Instagram Stories**: image + link sticker
    - **Local-life apps (Karrot)**: image + key details (the post's share buttons can copy a ready-made blurb)
    - **Naver neighborhood cafes**: image attached + post body

    If [your post is already up](https://findmypet.platformholder.site?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=fmp-launch-2026q2&utm_content=flyer-step5), every share is just the same single link.

    Time: 15 min
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Two things left after you find them

1. **Flip the post to `FOUND`** from your post list on the profile page (`/profile`). Duplicate tips stop, and anyone who bookmarked the post is notified of the status change.
2. **Collect the flyers.** Work down the map you built in step 5 and tick each spot off, so nothing gets left behind.

Cleaning up the paper is part of the job.

## Wrap-up — paper and digital cover each other's gaps

Flyers alone aren't enough. Digital alone isn't either.

Paper reaches the people who don't use apps; digital keeps the information current. A QR code is what connects the two.
