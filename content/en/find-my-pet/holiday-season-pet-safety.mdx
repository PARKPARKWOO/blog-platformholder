Lunar New Year, summer travel season, fireworks at midnight on New Year's Eve.

These are the windows when the risk of losing a pet climbs. The reasons are clear: **visitors, fireworks, unfamiliar places, long drives, and a front door that keeps opening** — variables that usually arrive separately, all at once.

## Why the risk climbs in these windows

Because the door opens more often, the stimulation is louder, and **the owner's attention is at its most divided**.

The paths are consistent:

- The front door opens for guests and the pet slips out **in under a second**
- The leash slips during a car trip or at a rest stop
- Fireworks, thunder, or loud music trigger a flight response
- They get lost on **unfamiliar streets or trails** while travelling

Holidays in particular stack family gatherings, cooking, driving, and hosting on top of each other. Same home, same animal — only the conditions changed.

<Callout type="warning" title="The most common pattern">
"Just a moment putting on shoes at the door" — it happens in under a second.
The more guests in a day, the more often that second arrives.
</Callout>

## 5-step prep (10 minutes)

Good habits any time, but worth a check right before holidays or travel.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Step 1 — Check the ID tag">
    - Not worn down or about to fall off
    - The engraved phone number is **the one you use now** (often missed after a move or number change)
    - Name and contact are enough; skip the home address for privacy

    Time: 1 min
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 2 — Bring microchip registration up to date">
    Look up your record on [animal.go.kr](https://www.animal.go.kr), Korea's national animal protection system.

    Most commonly missed:
    - New address after a move never reflected
    - Phone number changed but not updated

    **The implant is not the point — the registry entry is.** A shelter or vet can scan the chip and still fail to reach you if the number on file is old.

    If the pet isn't registered yet, this is a good moment to handle it. Eligibility, procedure, and fees vary by local authority, so check the current guidance on animal.go.kr.

    Time: 3–5 min
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 3 — Save 3–5 recent photos to the phone itself">
    What you need right after a loss is **a recent photo you can pull up instantly**.

    Required:
    - **Front face** (the identification anchor)
    - **Side full body** (build, coat pattern)
    - **Distinctive feature close-up** (spots, scars, tail colour)

    Don't rely only on the cloud — keep them in the **local photo library** too. Signal may be poor wherever you lose them.

    Time: 2 min
  </Step>

    Time: 2 min
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 5 — A 30-minute walk right before guests or departure">
    On high-stimulation days, baseline arousal is already higher than usual.

    A **30-minute walk** before guests arrive (or before you leave) burns off the excess and reduces door-rushing, barking, and flight responses.

    If a walk isn't possible, a scent game or hidden-treat session works as a substitute.

    Time: 30 min (day-of)
  </Step>
</Steps>

## If it does happen — the first 30 minutes

Even with all the prep, things happen. A short order to follow:

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| 0–5 min | Check hiding spots within 30m of the last known point — under cars, beneath stairs, inside planters — using name calls and treat sounds |
| 5–10 min | Alert family and friends; split into different directions |
| 10–15 min | [File a missing post on Find-My-Pet](https://findmypet.platformholder.site?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=fmp-launch-2026q2&utm_content=holiday-mid-cta) — photos, map pin, contact |
| 15–30 min | Share the post link in KakaoTalk groups, neighborhood OpenChat, and local communities |

If you're travelling, pin the coordinates to **where they actually disappeared, not your home**. Listings and maps surface by location, so a wrong pin means the neighbors who could help never see it.

If days pass without a result, work the shelter side too. Subscribing to a region also gets you notified when new listings appear there.
