On the walk home, an alley, a small dog wandering with no leash on.

You can't tell whether it's lost or just slipped away from its owner a minute ago. Approach and it might bolt; take it home and you have no idea what comes next. So you hesitate a few seconds, walk on — and **it stays with you all day.**

Here's how to use those few seconds instead.

## The moment you run toward them, they get further away

This is the most common mistake. Moving fast or calling out loudly pushes an already frightened animal **further away, often in a worse direction** — this is how they end up funnelled toward traffic.

So the first move isn't catching. It's **recording**. Keep your distance and take one or two photos, and you'll have the colour, size and collar details you need to compare against a missing post later.

The second mistake is the opposite one: taking them home because you feel for them. That quietly removes the animal from the exact places the owner is looking — **shelter notices and missing-post boards.**

## The fastest route to the owner is a post that's already up

Someone may already be out looking, with a post filed. One line — **"I saw them here"** — tells that person which streets to walk tonight.

[Find-My-Pet](https://findmypet.platformholder.site?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=fmp-launch-2026q2&utm_content=stray-intro) offers that connection three ways.

- **Combined search** — one breed or place name covers both missing posts and animals in shelter care. No login needed
- **Near-me radius filter** — missing posts within 1 km, 3 km, 5 km or 10 km of where you stand
- **Sighting report** — pin the location on the post and the owner gets an in-app notification

<Callout type="note" title="Your personal details don't go to the owner">
A report carries **a location and a note**. You don't have to publish a phone number. If you want to say more, the post's OpenChat link connects you with the owner one to one.
</Callout>

## The three-minute sequence

<Steps>
  <Step title="Step 1 — Distance, then one or two photos">
    Don't run, don't shout. Crouch low and stop.

    - One front shot and one side shot make comparison easy
    - Note the collar, ID tag, clothing, coat colour and rough size
    - If you have a treat, **place it down and step back** rather than throwing it

    Time: 30 sec
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 2 — Check the collar and ID tag">
    If you can safely get close, look for an ID tag.

    If a number is engraved on it, **calling from where you stand** is the fastest possible outcome. Plenty of cases end right here.

    Time: 30 sec
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 3 — Search the neighbourhood's missing posts">
    Type a breed or a place name into [Find-My-Pet search](https://findmypet.platformholder.site/search?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=fmp-launch-2026q2&utm_content=stray-step3).

    - Search works without a login
    - Results split into **missing** and **in shelter care** tabs
    - A single word — a breed, a neighbourhood name — is enough

    Time: 30 sec
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 4 — Nothing matching? Narrow by location">
    When you're unsure what to type, location narrows faster than words.

    Above the [home list](https://findmypet.platformholder.site?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=fmp-launch-2026q2&utm_content=stray-step4), the **near me** row offers `1km` `3km` `5km` `10km`. Allow browser location access and only nearby missing posts remain. Start at 1 km and widen if nothing shows.

    Time: 30 sec
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 5 — Match found? File the sighting">
    Open the post and compare the photos and markings. If it looks right, go to the **sighting** section lower down and press the report button.

    - It needs a **Kakao login** and **browser location permission**
    - The pin uses **your current coordinates**, which is why reporting from the spot is the accurate way
    - Already moved on? Write the location into the note
    - Example note: `car park at the apartments, 10 minutes ago, heading toward the main road`
    - Submit, and the owner receives an in-app notification

    Filed sightings stack up on a map under the post, which is how owners work out the direction of travel.

    Time: 1 min
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 6 — No matching post? Report it">
    Sometimes no post exists yet. Don't take the animal home to keep — report instead.

    1. Call the **animal protection division of your si/gun/gu district office** or a **local shelter**
    2. Give them the location and the animal's condition
    3. If the animal is on a dangerous road or appears injured, call **119** as well

    A reported animal appears in the public notices, and that's where owners are looking.

    Time: 2 min
  </Step>
</Steps>

## After you report, if you want to know how it ended

It's normal to keep thinking about it.

Tap the **star (bookmark)** at the top of the post and you'll get an in-app notification when its status changes to `found`. You get to know how it turned out.

<Callout type="tip" title="Notifications live inside the site">
Find-My-Pet notifications appear on the **bell icon** at the top of the screen and refresh every 30 seconds. Recent ones open from the bell; the full history sits on the notifications page. There's no app to install.
</Callout>

## Wrap-up — one more pair of eyes changes the odds

An owner walking all day can only cover so many alleys. What fills the rest is **whoever happened to walk past at that moment.**

If that was you today, three minutes is enough.
