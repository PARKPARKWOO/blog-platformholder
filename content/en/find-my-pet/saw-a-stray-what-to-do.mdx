A loose animal is not automatically an animal with no owner. The first need is not an ownership guess. It is **safety and an accurate observation record**.

## Do not approach when the location is unsafe

Near traffic, railways, or construction, do not chase or attempt capture. From a safe place, record the time, map location, and direction and notify the responsible authority. Keep your distance whenever approach feels unsafe.

When possible, take a photo and note the collar, clothing, and distinctive markings. Keep "probably abandoned" and other conclusions out of the record.

## Check the ID tag and public missing posts

Only check an ID tag if approaching is safe. Then use [Find-My-Pet's public search](https://findmypet.platformholder.site/search?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=fmp-sighting-2026q3&utm_content=stray-search) by area, breed, and identifying details. Lists, details, and combined search work without sign-in.

For a matching post, sign in with Kakao and file the time, place, and direction as a sighting. The form uses browser location permission, and a submitted report sends the owner an in-app notification. Bookmarking the post can also notify you of a later status change.

## File an official report when nothing matches

[Article 39 of Korea's current Animal Protection Act](https://www.law.go.kr/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1028318771) names the local government and animal shelter as reporting routes for a lost or abandoned animal.

Korea's Ministry of Agriculture provides [24-hour online reporting through the national system and the 1577-0954 connection line](https://mafra.go.kr/bbs/home/792/577317/artclView.do). Its guidance says an animal personally rescued should still be reported and handed over through the official shelter process.

See the [five-step lost-pet response checklist](/en/blog/find-my-pet/lost-pet-5-step-guide) for the owner-side context. After reporting, use [regional shelter-notice checks](/en/blog/find-my-pet/check-shelter-notices-by-region) to follow public notices.

One clear photo with time, place, and direction can help. You do not need to guess ownership or put yourself in danger.
