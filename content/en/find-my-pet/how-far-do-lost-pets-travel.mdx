Standing on the spot where your pet vanished, one question blocks everything else: **which way, and how far?**

Toward the main road, or deeper into the alleys? Is 500 m enough, or are they already in the next neighbourhood? Move before you answer that and you'll walk the same street three times — and when family arrives to help, "go check over there" means something different to each of them.

## More people doesn't mean more ground

A search widens by **non-overlapping area**, not by headcount.

Three people walking the same main road cover far less than three people each taking a different direction once. But zones split verbally almost always overlap, because "near the park" and "behind the apartments" mean different things to different people.

There's a second problem. **Dogs and cats need different search areas from the start.** Dogs tend to range further as time passes, while a startled cat often hides close by and barely moves for days. Search both the same way and you'll go too wide for one and too narrow for the other.

## We show this as three bands on a map

When you file a missing post on [Find-My-Pet](https://findmypet.platformholder.site?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=fmp-launch-2026q2&utm_content=radius-intro), the post's detail page draws a **search area** map.

It combines the species and breed with **how much time has passed since the loss**, and overlays three bands:

- **Core** — the highest-probability band. Sweep this on foot
- **Likely** — next priority. Cover it with a bike or car
- **Possible** — the outer edge. Cover it with flyers and online sharing

The method differs by species too. **For dogs, the area is drawn along the real road network** — what's actually reachable on foot — so directions cut off by a river or a major road aren't shaded in as if they were open. **Cats and other animals get a flat-terrain circle estimate.** The legend under the map tells you which method produced the shape you're looking at.

<Callout type="important" title="This is an order of operations, not a boundary">
The search area is an **estimate**. Don't use it to rule out everything outside the outer band.
Use it only to decide **what to sweep first**.
</Callout>

## Do this today

<Steps>
  <Step title="Step 1 — File the post with the exact spot pinned">
    Sign in to [Find-My-Pet](https://findmypet.platformholder.site?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=fmp-launch-2026q2&utm_content=radius-step1) with Kakao and file the missing post.

    - Typing the address into **place** moves the map there
    - Tap the map to correct the exact point. That coordinate becomes the centre of the search area
    - Pick the **species** (dog, cat, other) and the **breed** — 30 dog breeds, 15 cat breeds and 5 others are available, and the choice feeds the calculation
    - Enter the **time of loss** accurately. Elapsed time drives both the bands and the guidance

    Time: 5 min
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 2 — Open the search area on the post">
    Once filed, open the post's detail page. Below the photos and details sits the **search area** map.

    - Core, Likely and Possible are overlaid from the inside out
    - The legend states **real road network** or **flat-terrain circle estimate**
    - The zoom level adjusts itself to the size of the area

    Time: 1 min
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 3 — Split people and zones, starting from Core">
    Assign roles while looking at the same map.

    1. **Core** — on foot. Under cars, narrow alleys, stairwells, inside planters, AC unit alcoves
    2. **Likely** — by bike or car, focusing on park entrances, shop notice boards and vet clinics
    3. **Possible** — don't walk it. Cover it with flyers and online sharing

    Helpers only need **the post link**. The detail page opens without a login, so whoever you send it to sees the same map and can claim a zone — "I'll take the north side of Core."

    Time: 5 min
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 4 — For cats, work the gaps inside first">
    Cat posts show a note under the map telling you to start with the tight radius.

    - Prioritise spaces a body barely fits into: storage rooms, under cars, AC alcoves, stairwells, wall gaps
    - Keep noise down and re-check **the same spots** during quiet early hours
    - Use a familiar treat bag sound or their usual blanket

    Do a few more passes close in before widening.

    Time: 30 min per pass
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 5 — Re-plan around the elapsed-time banner">
    Above the map, a banner shows **how much time has passed** and what works at that stage. The wording changes over time and the map's shading fades — a signal to stop staring at the radius alone.

    After 72 hours, the post also shows a block pointing you at:

    - Checking public shelter notices daily
    - Contacting shelters and vet clinics near the loss point about temporary care
    - Reposting to local communities and social feeds
    - Flyer spots that stay up for weeks (24h convenience stores, vet waiting rooms)

    Time: 5 min once a day
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Time passing changes the method, not the odds

After a few days the radius naturally grows. Sweeping that whole circle isn't something a handful of people can do.

So at that stage you switch from **covering more ground yourself** to **adding more eyes that cover it for you**: checking shelter notices daily, leaving flyers where they'll survive, reposting in neighbourhood communities. Keep one post link current and every channel carries the same up-to-date information.

<Callout type="tip" title="When tips come in">
A sighting report shows up in the **notification bell** at the top of the site, refreshed every 30 seconds. Edit your post to add any new sighting location or detail — everyone holding the link then sees the update.
</Callout>

## Wrap-up — the map gives you an order, not an answer

What the search area map tells you isn't "they're here." It's **"start here."**

That alone changes the first hour: you know which way to go, you have a zone to hand to whoever came to help, and you stop walking the same street twice.
