"How many kilometers should I search?" sounds precise, but one number can close a search too early.

## The research shows differences, not a universal rule

A [retrospective survey covering 1,210 missing-cat cases](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5789300/) found different found distances with different outdoor histories. The sample was self-selected, and many respondents were recalling older events. Its percentiles and distances cannot predict one cat in Korea.

A [US household survey of lost dogs and cats](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4494319/) also reported different recovery methods by species. Neither study says every pet hides nearby or every dog travels far.

## Start at the actual last-seen point

Pin where the pet was last seen, not the center of a street address. Record the time, direction, species and breed, and previous outdoor access.

[Create a Find-My-Pet post with the location and species details](https://findmypet.platformholder.site/register?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=fmp-search-area-2026q3&utm_content=radius-register) to view Core, Likely, and Possible reference areas on the detail page. Dogs use a road-network reachable area when available. Cats, other animals, and routing failures use a circular estimate.

<Callout type="note" title="The map is not a boundary">
Elapsed-time guidance and reachable areas help choose where to start. If a new sighting falls outside them, give the sighting's time, place, and direction priority.
</Callout>

## Update outward from the immediate area

Divide the area around the last-seen point before choosing one distant destination. Then mark roads, waterways, railways, fences, and access points that may block or channel movement.

Record who checked each area and when. Keep an unverified relay separate from a confirmed sighting so the team does not repeat one route or exclude another without evidence.

Use the [five-step lost-pet response checklist](/en/blog/find-my-pet/lost-pet-5-step-guide) for the full response. When the search crosses district lines, continue with [checking shelter notices in adjacent regions](/en/blog/find-my-pet/check-shelter-notices-by-region).

A search radius is not a number you set once. It is a working area redrawn from the last-seen point, terrain, and new evidence.
