Checking shelter notices is not just swiping through photographs. You need to read the **dates, found location, and shelter contact together**.

## Keep the two legal periods separate

[Article 40 of Korea's current Animal Protection Act](https://www.law.go.kr/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1028318771) requires the covered animals in local-government care to be publicly notified without delay for **at least seven days**. Article 43 on the same page says the local government may acquire ownership if the owner remains unknown **ten days after the notice date**.

Seven days and ten days are separate legal rules. Do not reduce them to "the shelter period is always ten days." Read the notice and ask the shelter.

## Move from the loss district to adjacent districts

[Find-My-Pet's regional shelter notices](https://findmypet.platformholder.site/abandonment/region?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=fmp-shelter-check-2026q3&utm_content=region-page) show active notices and shelter contacts by province and district without sign-in.

Check in this order:

1. the district containing the loss point
2. neighboring districts
3. places connected by roads, waterways, or trails

The guide to [expanding a search from terrain rather than a fixed radius](/en/blog/find-my-pet/how-far-do-lost-pets-travel) explains why.

## Do not decide from a photo alone

For a possible match, compare the notice date and found location, sex, estimated age, collar, and distinctive markings. Then prepare the notice number, registration number, and current photos and **call the shelter listed on the notice**.

A closed notice does not prove return, adoption, or another outcome. Find-My-Pet separates active and closed notices, but closed detail pages direct readers to confirm the current status with the shelter.

## Use both a checking record and alerts

Record the districts and the time you checked them. This reduces repeated work and makes an omitted neighboring area visible. Signed-in users can subscribe to in-app alerts for new notices in a selected area. Alerts are supplemental; a specific sighting should still go directly to the shelter and responsible authority.
